| 24 | An approver decides their own request (self-approval) or a delegated service identity approves on behalf of the actor that requested the action, defeating separation of duties | Low (by design) | Critical — undermines the entire governance model's purpose | `recordApprovalDecision` explicitly compares the decider against `action_snapshot`'s requesting user, requesting tenant agent, and delegated service identity before accepting any decision — checked in code (`separationOfDuties.ts`), not left to convention (`PHASE_4_GOVERNANCE_APPROVALS.md` §7) |
| 25 | A tenant policy override weakens a platform-mandatory security policy (e.g. lowers a required-approval count, downgrades an effect toward `ALLOW`, or lowers a risk level below the platform's classification floor) | Medium if not structurally prevented | Critical — the exact failure mode the "tenant overrides must never weaken mandatory policies/classifications" rule exists to prevent | `PolicyOverrideDocumentSchema` has no field capable of loosening an effect or reducing a requirement (only additive/tightening fields exist); overrides targeting an `override_policy = 'immutable'` policy version are rejected at write time; the merge algorithm always picks the _most restrictive_ effect (`BLOCK > DENY > ESCALATE > REQUIRE_APPROVAL > ALLOW`) across all matches and the _highest_ risk level (classification version as floor, policies can only raise it via `max()`), so even a hypothetical bad override can't soften a mandatory `BLOCK`/`DENY` or lower a risk floor (`PHASE_4_GOVERNANCE_APPROVALS.md` §1, §3c, §4) |
| 26 | An approval request is resumed twice (e.g. a live `recordApprovalDecision` call and a concurrent recovery sweep both act on the same `APPROVED` request), duplicating a governed action's effect | Low (by design) | High — duplicate financial/external/destructive action execution | Two independent atomic-conditional-UPDATE gates: `approval_requests.continuation_committed` flips exactly once, and `workflow-engine`'s own step-status-gated transition is itself exactly-once — either gate alone prevents double execution, both exist as defense in depth (`PHASE_4_GOVERNANCE_APPROVALS.md` §10) |
